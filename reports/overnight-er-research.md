# overnight entity-resolution research: offline and leaderboard macro f0.5

- **research question:** why can offline and leaderboard scores diverge in this business entity-resolution challenge, and which tests are most likely to improve high-precision macro f0.5?
- **scope:** the supplied challenge, current repository evidence, and published primary sources; no business-identity lookup or external entity data was used or proposed for the pipeline.
- **date:** 2026-09-26.

## conclusion

**the original baseline has an observed offline/public difference of 0.0115015568; the numbers do not establish its cause.** its locked full-labeled-target-pool audit is 0.9755015568, and its team-reported public score is 0.964. upgraded v1's team-reported public score is 0.969. the offline audit and public leaderboard use different evaluation populations, and upgraded v1 has no recorded full-pool locked-audit score here. model changes, cutoff selection, negative-pool effects, and country shift remain **hypotheses, not diagnosed causes**. the first priority is a controlled full-pool comparison with the challenge's exact per-reference metric; a larger embedding or language model cannot substitute for that comparison. ## confirmed repository facts, not hypotheses

1. **the unit of scoring is a source 1 set.** each source 1 record can have zero or many source 2/3 matches; a correctly empty set gets 1, while any prediction for a true singleton gets 0. for nonempty truth, `f0.5 = 1.25 * tp / (1.25 * tp + fp + 0.25 * fn)`; a false positive has four times the denominator weight of a false negative. the score is averaged over *all source 1 records*. generic pairwise f0.5, micro f0.5, and a library's per-label `average='macro'` are different objectives. the repository's `score` function implements the specified empty-set convention and mean across reference ids. [rules](../Ml_Challenge.txt#L3-L4) · [metric](../Ml_Challenge.txt#L112-L125) · [exact per-anchor implementation](../src/eda.py#L28-L31) · [scorer](../src/validate.py#L204-L225) · [fbeta api: macro means per label](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.fbeta_score.html)
2. **offline and public scores are distinct measurements.** the baseline scored all 10,320,219 labeled targets; it selected `target_top1_then_threshold` at 0.55275691 on fold 0 and obtained macro f0.5 **0.97550156**, link precision **0.98750**, and link recall **0.95688** on untouched fold 1. its plain-threshold fold-0 macro score was 0.97251640 versus 0.97531770 for target-top1. the team-reported baseline public score is **0.964**, an observed offline/public difference of **0.0115015568**. upgraded v1 selected 0.8 using a *selected-query pair* diagnostic and received a **team-reported** public macro score of **0.969**; no upgraded full-pool locked-audit score is recorded here. the diagnostic's pair f0.5 of 0.98216 at 0.8 is not a comparable offline macro score; format validation is not an accuracy test. 3. **there is a real, measurable shift but no france ground truth.** train source 1 has 1,323,633 us and 883,188 india records; test has 663,106 us, 809,986 india, and 259,452 france records (about 15% of test anchors). train labels contain 123,247 singleton anchors and 2,681,854 targets not linked to any train anchor. test singleton prevalence, target ownership, public-set country composition, and france accuracy are unknown. the test target-to-anchor ratio is higher than the train ratio; **inference:** this can increase retrieval competition but does *not* establish a change in true link counts. 4. **grouping is present; all leakage risks are not thereby eliminated.** `src/data.py` assigns fold 2 for fitting, fold 0 for tuning, and fold 1 for audit at the source1 reference level; known aliases retain the same owner. full-pool calibration includes all labelled targets, including unlinked distractors, and selects a cutoff on fold 0 only. our team found that sampled-query evaluation could omit wrong links into unsampled references, while sampled reference aggregates could reveal selected owners. no repository result proves that related different source1 records or shared name/address families are independent across folds. 5. **candidate recall and final accuracy are separate.** on 6,925 selected india positive targets, lexical plus e5 plus qwen at dense width 100 retrieved **0.99538** of true-owner pairs *before* the final gate; adding e5-large retrieved five additional positives in that selected experiment. on a selected two-country diagnostic, the gate's top-three-per-target candidates retained **0.98402** of positive owner links, before final scoring and rejection. neither number is a full-population oracle macro score or an estimate of france performance. the submitted final candidate file is the actual post-gate set scored by the neural matcher, not the wider initial retrieval union. ## what the primary research supports, and what it does not

- **splits and distractors.** the scikit-learn [grouped cross-validation guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html#cross-validation-iterators-for-grouped-data) says dependent examples must be kept in the same fold; its [leakage guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html#data-leakage) also warns against learning transformations from evaluation data. the [ditto paper, benchmark section](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.00584v3#S4.SS1) describes labeled pairs sampled *after blocking*, not this challenge's complete open-world reference population. **inference:** optimizing pair accuracy on enriched or owner-selected samples can miscalibrate false merges from millions of genuinely unmatched or competing targets. existing grouped folds help, but full target-pool scoring and additional signature-family stress splits answer different questions.
- **blocking is a hard ceiling, not a scorer feature.** the [filtering benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.12521v5#S3) defines pair completeness as the fraction of gold pairs entering the candidate set and explains why omitted pairs cannot be recovered by a pair classifier; it explicitly distinguishes nearest-neighbor retrieval recall from entity-match recall. [dial](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.03986v2#S3.SS2) separates high-recall blocker training from precision-oriented matcher training: its hard near-duplicate negatives helped the matcher but could harm blocker coverage. **inference:** measure both union-stage and *post-gate* truth retention, then calculate the perfect-classifier set-score ceiling for the exported candidate set before spending effort on a new reranker.
- **decoding and calibration must match the evaluation unit.** scikit-learn's [decision-threshold guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/classification_threshold.html) separates ranking scores from the decision rule and warns against fitting a threshold on the model's own training data. its [calibration guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html) recommends held-out predictions for probability calibration. a [published f1 threshold analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/1402.1892v2#S7) shows that empirically selected thresholds can overfit; its special `optimal threshold = f1/2` result is for calibrated **f1**, **not** this per-reference f0.5 with empty-set rewards and target-owner constraints. **inference:** directly tune the actual source 1 set objective on fold 0 using realistic negatives; test both no-match decisions and target-top1 conflicts; keep fold 1 locked.
- **embeddings and larger language models have bounded roles.** e5's [first-party model card](https://huggingface.co/intfloat/multilingual-e5-base#usage) requires `query:`/`passage:` prefixes even for non-english text and warns that low-resource languages can degrade. [ditto](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.00584v3#S3) supports jointly encoded record-pair classification and harder examples, while noting that text augmentation can change a match label. a [2026 controlled study, preprint under review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24688) reports cross-encoder advantages, non-universal gains from generative matchers, and larger-model shortcut learning. the [qwen3 reranker card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B#using-sentence-transformers) gives **raw relevance logits** under a *web-search* default prompt; a sigmoid is not evidence of calibrated business-identity probability. **inference:** evaluate any eligible local model on the **same** retained hard pairs and held-out reference sets; it cannot recover pairs never sent to it, and generic retrieval benchmarks cannot guarantee this task's precision.
- **consistency is a diagnostic, not permission to merge everything.** [transclean's original paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04006v1#S4.SS1) uses contradictory implied links to locate likely multi-source false positives. the train labels assign each linked target one owner, while a reference may have many aliases in both target sources. **inference:** compare target-top1 conflict handling with plain thresholding, and use disagreement within predicted groups for error review; do not impose one match per target *source* or blindly close connected components. ## ranked falsifiable experiments

all experiments below use only supplied records and labels, fixed model versions, the existing reference-grouped fit/tune/audit protocol, and the exact source 1 scorer. use fold 0 for development; predeclare a small finalist set and inspect fold 1 only for its locked comparison. report paired per-anchor score differences and uncertainty, not only an aggregate or a pairwise precision figure.

| priority | controlled experiment and evidence to collect | result that changes the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | score the *upgraded* model against **every train target**, including unmatched and other-owner targets; independently tune baseline and upgrade on fold 0, freeze thresholds/decoders, compare on fold 1 with identical anchor ids. break down false merges, singleton errors, missing links, us/india, s2/s3, and score margins. | if upgrade loses on the paired locked audit, the provisional cutoff or changed gate/retriever is suspect; if it does not, the public gap remains unexplained and country/test shift becomes a stronger hypothesis. this does not reveal france labels. |
| 2 | for the same folds, account for each true link at lexical/dense union, post-gate top 1/3/5, and final acceptance; compute **oracle source 1 macro f0.5** when predicting exactly the true links retained at each stage, with correct singleton scores; include all-links-per-anchor coverage, missed-link slices, and extra scored pairs. | an oracle below 0.998 rules out 0.998 for that candidate stage **on the evaluated fold**, even with a perfect reranker; it does not establish the unseen test oracle. if oracle clears it but the model does not, prioritize scoring/decoding; increase width only when recovered links survive the gate and improve end-to-end audit. |
| 3 | run owner-disjoint stress validations that retain full-country distractors, then stratify by common-name collisions, same-address competitors, missing addresses, script change, and link count. compare smaller versus full known training target pools without dropping known negatives; test held-out name/address-signature families and us-to-india / india-to-us transfer separately. | degradation with distractor density supports sample-selection bias; degradation on family or source-shift tests supports generalization risk. no synthetic france labels or claim of measured france f0.5. |
| 4 | on *fixed* post-gate pairs, compare baseline scoring with retrained hard-negative scoring (other-owner near matches, shared-name/address cases, unmatched targets); separately compare global cutoff, calibrated no-match gate, and target-top1 decoding, optimizing fold-0 **set macro**. keep retriever and candidates fixed. | promote only if paired locked-audit gain survives singleton and country checks; poor precision on genuine distractors falsifies a sampled-pair improvement. keep hard-negative blocker fine-tuning separate from matcher training. |
| 5 | only if step 2 shows adequate candidate oracle and step 4 leaves a resolvable scoring tail, compare an eligible locally run cross-encoder/reranker against the same hard candidates, with task-specific instruction, frozen splits, and exact end-to-end set score. | retain only a measured held-out set-score gain at acceptable error rates; larger model size, benchmark ranking, raw logits, and a five-link retrieval gain alone do not meet the test. |

## what 99.8% would require

**0.998 macro f0.5 is a stretch target, not an evidence-backed projection.** the baseline audit's 0.97550156 leaves roughly **0.02450** mean per-anchor loss; reaching 0.998 allows just **0.002**, about **12.25 times less residual loss** on a comparable population. on 1,732,544 test anchors, that allowance is about **3,465 anchor-loss units**: each wrong merge into a true singleton uses one whole unit, even if every other anchor were perfect. these are arithmetic consequences of the supplied metric, *not* a forecast of unseen labels or an exact allowed count of mistakes. with roughly 15% of test anchors in unlabeled france, even perfect performance elsewhere would require france macro f0.5 above about **0.9866**; this is a conditional illustration, not a france estimate. a measured candidate oracle below 0.998 on a held-out population rules it out **on that population** for that frozen candidate set, but does not prove the test oracle; an oracle above it still would not imply a model can achieve it. ## assumptions, uncertainty, and practical implications

- the public scores 0.964 for the baseline and 0.969 for upgraded v1 are **team-reported**, not independently verified here. the baseline's 0.0115015568 offline/public difference is descriptive across different evaluation populations, not a causal finding. the organizer's public/private subset labels, country mix, test singleton rate, and france match truth are unavailable.
